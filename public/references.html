Personal portfolio references

Inspected on 28 October 2026. This is a deliberately varied reference set, not an objective ranking. Observations come from the live websites: published page content and browser views. The inspection covers the visible composition and selected interactions, not a full accessibility or performance audit. No reference assets, code, copy, or layouts are to be reproduced.

1. Bruno Simon

URL: https://bruno-simon.com/

Observed: The current entry scene places a small vehicle on a warm, stylized 3D island against a spatial grid, with a clear start prompt. The site's own interface describes a drivable portfolio world and exposes keyboard, touch, and gamepad controls, a map, audio, quality, and respawn options. The browser reached the entry scene; the whole world was not playtested.

Lesson: A portfolio can demonstrate its author's capabilities through its navigation. A novel spatial interface still needs explicit controls and a straightforward way to recover orientation.

For Ivan: Make a knowledge instrument respond to selection and dragging, with project labels immediately available. A full driving game would add a second product to maintain without improving the presentation of his systems work.

2. Brittany Chiang

URL: https://brittanychiang.com/

Observed: A dark, restrained two-column composition keeps the name, role, section navigation, and social links together. The reading column presents a short introduction, dated experience, concrete responsibilities, project descriptions, and technology labels. The page has a skip-to-content link and a direct résumé link.

Lesson: A visitor can establish professional fit before opening a project. Hierarchy and clear writing do substantial work without visual noise.

For Ivan: Put a precise role, a short statement of what he builds, and direct work/contact links in the first viewport. Give each project a purpose, Ivan's contribution, and a route to evidence.

3. Dennis Snellenberg

URL: https://dennissnellenberg.com/

Observed: A full-height photographic hero pairs a very large, horizontally moving name with a short role, location, and a three-item navigation. The published page then moves to a compact list of recent work, an about link, and a prominent contact invitation.

Lesson: One dominant composition feels more confident than several equally loud panels. Large typography can create motion and identity while supporting very little copy.

For Ivan: Use oversized, carefully spaced type and a single focal 3D object. Preserve generous empty space around the object and keep the primary navigation short.

4. Bartosz Kolenda

URL: https://bartoszkolenda.com/

Observed: The garden designer's homepage uses deep green, pale yellow type, an oversized mixed serif/sans headline, and staggered project photography. A compact navigation sits near the bottom of the viewport. The site names services and explains a six-step process.

Lesson: A personal site can have an editorial visual identity tied directly to its author's practice. Type contrast and asymmetry provide richness without needing extra effects.

For Ivan: Pair an expressive display face with sober interface typography. Let a few original diagrams and project scenes establish his practice instead of applying one generic card pattern everywhere.

5. Rauno Freiberg

URLs: https://rauno.me/ · https://rauno.me/craft

Observed: The homepage presents a large identity card with emphatic black type and a bright yellow circular field. The Craft archive names small, specific interface studies, including toolbar morphing, spatial tooltips, graph sliders, precision sliders, and radial menus, alongside dates and links to prototypes or production work.

Lesson: Craft becomes credible through small, inspectable examples. Each interaction can have a single point rather than being part of a continuous spectacle.

For Ivan: Use a few deliberate responses: an orbit that settles after a drag, a selected node that brings its context forward, and a clear return to the overview. Demonstrate the work behind an effect with a project detail or execution trace.

6. Josh W. Comeau

URLs: https://www.joshwcomeau.com/ · https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/interactive-guide-to-flexbox/

Observed: The homepage combines a playful illustrated header and character with a highly readable article feed. The Flexbox guide interleaves explanations with controls and diagrams: container-width sliders, layout selectors, and editable examples sit next to the concepts they explain.

Lesson: Interaction is strongest when it teaches the subject. Personality and serious technical depth can coexist if the reading structure remains calm.

For Ivan: Include one small knowledge-flow or physics demonstration in a case study. Let a visitor trigger a trace or apply an impulse and see the result, so the interaction reveals engineering judgment.

7. David Milan

URL: https://www.davidmilan.com/

Observed: An original, hand-drawn-looking wordmark fills the top of the page over a sky image. Below it, an image-led gallery combines lettering, collage, and motion work at a large scale. The descriptive categories and contact surface are brief. This is an artist reference; no affiliation with Lusion is implied.

Lesson: A distinctive identity comes from authored visual material, not just a fashionable effect. Large work samples can explain a creative practice quickly.

For Ivan: Create original linework and project-specific artifacts: an etched knowledge diagram, a propulsion schematic, or a Book of Shadow seal. Keep these tied to the subject of the project rather than using symbols as arbitrary decoration.

8. Hakim El Hattab

URL: https://hakim.se/

Observed: A large, plain introduction establishes the person and current product. A dense visual collection then links useful applications, interface experiments, animations, and games. Each item has a concise description; the site distinguishes personal work from past employment and includes a compact Q&A.

Lesson: Breadth reads as a coherent practice when the projects are easy to scan and their intent is explicit. A working experiment is stronger evidence than a decorative skill meter.

For Ivan: Present AI tools, Magik Bot, physics games, and Worklife as different applications of one systems-building practice. Use real links, short descriptions, and honest project status instead of numerical self-ratings.

9. Jesse Zhou

URL: https://www.jesse-zhou.com/

Observed: After an explicit Start button, the browser renders a compact neon ramen-shop diorama in a dark environment. The shop's signpost labels Projects, Articles, About Me, and Credits, making the scene itself carry section names. The scene was entered and visually inspected; the complete project navigation was not tested.

Lesson: A single, richly composed place can create a memorable identity. Labels integrated into the scene give the fantasy a practical purpose.

For Ivan: One original observatory or knowledge engine is sufficient. Use a small family of labeled regions and project-specific transformations. Retain ordinary page navigation alongside the scene so reaching the work is immediate.

10. Seán Halpin

URL: https://www.seanhalpin.xyz/

Observed: A large personal greeting and short statement lead into substantial project previews. A compact top navigation separates Work, About, Play, and Notes, with a visible Contact action. The page explicitly labels a separate set of projects as In Progress.

Lesson: Playfulness works well when projects and their maturity are legible. Visitors can choose a direct professional path or spend time exploring.

For Ivan: Keep selected shipped work separate from experiments and research. A clear Projects route and contact action should always remain available, even when the 3D instrument is being explored.

An accessible historical mirror exposed the résumé text but lacked parts of the imagery, so it was not counted as one of the ten fully visual references above.

Original synthesis for Ivan

The direction is an interactive observatory of systems: a precise, tactile 3D instrument surrounded by an editorial portfolio. It connects the knowledge graphs of Vajra/Ghanta/Mudra, the procedural geometry of Magik Bot, the forces and trajectories of game simulation, and the human outcomes of Worklife. It is a new composition based on Ivan's work, not a collage of the reference layouts.

- Identity: A confident name and role at the first glance. Warm white text, graphite surroundings, a restrained warm metal accent, and one contrasting signal color. Original etched linework supplies character.

- Hero: One dimensional instrument with an orbiting knowledge structure. Pointer movement gives subtle parallax; dragging rotates it; selecting a labeled point changes the highlighted project and reveals a short description. There is no mandatory introduction before the work.

- Information: A short introduction, selected projects, a compact practice/tooling section, and contact. Project entries explain problem, contribution, and concrete outcome or current status. Technologies support the story instead of becoming the story.

- Depth: Project selection brings one idea into focus: a graph and visible execution steps for AI tools, an original circular manuscript study for Magik Bot, propulsion vectors for a game, and an opportunity/pipeline diagram for Worklife.

- Motion: Calm ambient motion and short, responsive transitions. The selected state, keyboard focus, and active project remain obvious. Motion reduction and narrow screens keep the same content available in a simpler composition.

- Restraint: Use one principal 3D scene and a few meaningful interactions. Avoid a long loader, a compulsory game, invented metrics, generic capability cards, and unrelated sci-fi ornament.

The useful combination is Bruno's demonstrative interaction, Brittany's professional clarity, Dennis and Bartosz's editorial hierarchy, Rauno's care for local responses, Josh and Hakim's inspectable explanations, and Jesse/David/Seán's personal visual authorship.
